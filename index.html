CuratED: Key Pharmacotherapy Updates of 2024

An infographic summary of pivotal guidelines, trials, and meta-analyses in emergency medicine pharmacotherapy.

Resuscitation

Acute Heart Failure Diuretics

  • For patients on oral loop diuretics, give an IV dose **1 to 2.5 times** the oral furosemide equivalent.
  • Higher initial doses (2.5x) may lead to greater fluid loss and symptom relief.
  • For diuretic-naïve patients, an initial IV furosemide dose of **40-80 mg** is common.

RSI: Ketamine vs Etomidate

A Bayesian meta-analysis showed an 83.2% probability that ketamine reduces mortality compared to etomidate in critically ill adults, but the result was **not statistically significant** (RR 0.93, 95% CrI 0.79-1.08). Choice should remain patient-specific.

Corticosteroids in Critical Illness

  • **Sepsis:** Conditional recommendation for corticosteroids in septic shock.
  • **ARDS:** Suggested for hospitalised adults (moderate certainty).
  • **Severe CAP:** Recommended for hospitalised adults (strong recommendation).

Trauma

Prehospital Fluids & Vasopressors

  • **Isotonic crystalloids** are the preferred prehospital fluid.
  • **Permissive hypotension** is reasonable in patients without TBI.
  • For TBI, target SBP **≥110 mmHg**.
  • Vasopressors are **not recommended** for routine use in traumatic haemorrhagic shock.

Antibiotic Prophylaxis

  • **Recommended for:** Penetrating trauma (head, chest, abdomen), open fractures.
  • **Not recommended for:** Non-operative blunt trauma, clean wounds.
  • **Open Fractures:** Prehospital administration of a first-generation cephalosporin is recommended if it doesn't delay transport.

TBI & Intracranial Haemorrhage

Seizure Prophylaxis in TBI

A shift in guidelines: Prophylactic anti-seizure medication (ASM) is now considered **optional** for moderate-severe TBI.

  • If used, **levetiracetam** is preferred over phenytoin.
  • A short course of **≤7 days** is recommended.

Tranexamic Acid (TXA)

  • Meta-analysis: TXA **did not reduce mortality** in TBI but did reduce haemorrhagic expansion.
  • Subgroup analysis: For CT-confirmed ICH, a prehospital **2g IV bolus of TXA** within 2 hours significantly lowered mortality compared to placebo or 1g+1g infusion.

Ischaemic Stroke

Tenecteplase vs Alteplase

Multiple trials (TASTE, ORIGINAL, ATTEST-2) confirm **Tenecteplase (TNK) 0.25 mg/kg is non-inferior** to Alteplase for AIS within 4.5 hours. A meta-analysis suggests TNK may be superior for functional outcomes (mRS 0-1).

IVT + Thrombectomy

The benefit of IV thrombolysis before thrombectomy is **highly time-dependent**. The greatest benefit is seen with the earliest possible administration. The TIMELESS trial showed **no benefit** for TNK in the 4.5 to 24-hour window for patients undergoing thrombectomy.

Thrombolysis in Minor Stroke

TEMPO-2 trial: In minor stroke (NIHSS 0-5) with occlusion, TNK showed **no benefit and potential harm** (increased ICH/mortality) compared to standard care. For non-LVO minor stroke, DAPT may be safer than alteplase.

Anticoagulation Reversal

Andexanet Alfa for DOAC Reversal

ANNEXA-I trial: For DOAC-associated ICH, Andexanet alfa showed **superior haemostatic efficacy** compared to usual care (PCC).

  • However, there was **no difference in 30-day mortality** and a **higher rate of thrombotic events** with Andexanet.

PCC Dosing for VKA Reversal

Meta-analyses suggest **fixed-dose PCC** is as effective as weight-based dosing for achieving an INR goal of <2.0. Fixed-dosing was associated with **lower mortality and fewer thromboembolic events**, making it an appealing alternative.

Toxicology & Miscellaneous

Opioid Reversal

For suspected opioid poisoning, adding **1600 µg IM naloxone** to titrated IV naloxone significantly reduced respiratory depression recurrence and the need for a continuous infusion. (Note: No fentanyl cases in study).

Geriatric Medication Safety

GEMS-Rx identified high-risk medications to **avoid prescribing at ED discharge** for older adults, including: benzodiazepines, first-gen antihistamines, muscle relaxants, and sulfonylureas.

Severe Agitation

ACEP policy update: For severe agitation in the ED, a combination of **droperidol and midazolam** offers the best balance. If a single agent is needed, **droperidol** is preferred. Ketamine use requires a controlled setting.
